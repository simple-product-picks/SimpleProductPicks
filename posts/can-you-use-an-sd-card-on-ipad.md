# Can You Use An SD Card On An iPad?

## Connect the card through a reader

The card goes into a card reader: you slot the SD card into the reader, then connect the reader to the iPad's charging port. From there iPadOS treats the card like any other external drive, and the Files app opens it. Which reader you need comes down to your iPad's port, covered in the last section below.

## Two ways the card shows up: Files and Photos

A connected card appears in two places. In the Files app, the card shows up as a location in the sidebar, next to iCloud Drive and On My iPad, so you can browse every folder, copy files off the card and move files onto it. This is the route for documents, music, a mix of file types, or photos you have already sorted into your own folders.

Photos is the camera route. Plug in a card that a camera wrote to and the Photos app can pull the shots straight into your library through an Import screen, where you tap Import All or pick individual frames first. Apple states the limit clearly: "Apple camera adapters can import only content that was captured by digital cameras." So a card full of PDFs or music will not appear on the Photos Import screen at all, though it still opens normally in Files.

## The card has to be formatted a way the iPad understands

Apple's iPad User Guide sets one firm rule for any external drive or card: "An external storage device must have only a single data partition, and it must be formatted as APFS, APFS (encrypted), macOS Extended (HFS+), exFAT (FAT64), FAT32, or FAT." A card split into two partitions, or formatted for something the list does not cover, will not show up. To change that, reformat the card, in the Files app on the iPad or on a computer.

None of this touches how fast the copy runs, which depends on the card's UHS class and is covered in the [UHS-I vs UHS-II explainer](uhs-i-vs-uhs-ii-explained.html).

## Match the reader to your iPad's port

The reader has to fit the iPad you own. Current iPads use USB-C and take a USB-C card reader, Apple's or another maker's. Older iPads use the Lightning connector and need a Lightning card reader or camera adapter instead.

If you want a reader that also keeps the iPad charged while it works, picking one with a pass-through charging port is the job of the [best USB-C SD card reader guide](best-usb-c-sd-card-reader-uk.html).

## FAQ

**Q: My iPad will not read the card, what is wrong?**
A: Work through three checks in order. First, open Files rather than Photos, since Photos only lists what a camera recorded. Second, look at the card's partitions and format against Apple's list above, and reformat it if either is off, which erases the card, so copy anything you need first. Third, make sure the reader's plug matches the iPad's port, USB-C or Lightning.

**Q: Can I use a microSD card on an iPad?**
A: Yes, in a microSD-to-SD adapter or a reader with a microSD slot.
