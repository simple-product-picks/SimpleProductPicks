# Why Does My Power Bank Turn Off?

## It reads a tiny current draw as nothing plugged in

Most power banks turn themselves off on purpose. A bank keeps an eye on how much current whatever you plug in is pulling, and when that draw stays very small it decides the job is finished and cuts the output. An earbuds case, a smartwatch, a fitness band and a nearly full phone all draw very little, so the bank mistakes them for an empty cable and shuts down after a few seconds. It is a built-in habit meant to save the bank's own charge, not a fault.

Anker puts a figure on it for its own banks, saying a connected device has to "draw at least 30-90mA to continue operation"; anything under that will not hold the output on.

## The fix is low-current (trickle) mode

Most banks that behave this way include a mode for small accessories. Anker calls it Trickle Charging Mode, "specifically designed for low-power consumption devices, such as earbuds, Bluetooth earphones, and smart wearable rings", toggled when you "double-press the power button"; it gives "a regulated, low-current output (less than 0.5A)" and "automatically turns off after 2 hours if no interaction occurs". Huawei documents the same control on its banks: "double-press the button to enter low-current charging mode" to charge "low-power devices, such as a band or Bluetooth earphones", then press again to exit.

The name and the exact button pattern vary by brand, so the step to find is in your own bank's manual. A bank with no such mode will keep cutting out on tiny loads, and there is no setting that stops it.

## When it will not charge anything at all

A bank that also dies on a phone is a different problem, and the usual causes are ordinary. The bank itself may be flat and need recharging first. The cable may be worn or damaged, which shows up as charging that starts and stops or never begins. The port on either end can be loose or packed with pocket lint. A bank that has grown hot will pause its output until it cools, through the same protection circuit. Swap in a known-good cable early, since a tired cable looks exactly like a dead bank.

## What to check before you buy one

A low-current mode is the feature that decides this, and the cheaper the bank the more often it is left out. ## FAQ

**Q: Why does my power bank turn off when charging my earbuds?**
A: An earbuds case draws too little current for the bank to count it as a device, so the bank cuts its output to save charge. Anker says its banks need a device drawing "at least 30-90mA to continue operation". Switch on the low-current or trickle mode if your bank has one.

**Q: How do I stop it cutting out on small devices?**
A: Use the bank's low-current mode. On Anker and Huawei banks you double-press the power button, and the mode has to be left running while the accessory charges. The exact press is in your bank's manual, and a bank without the mode cannot be stopped from cutting out.

**Q: Why will my power bank not charge my phone at all?**
A: That points away from the low-current cut-out and towards something simpler: a flat bank, a worn or damaged cable, a dirty or loose port, or heat pausing the output. Recharge the bank, change the cable and clean the port before blaming the battery.

**Q: Will it keep my earbuds topped up overnight?**
A: Not necessarily. Anker's trickle mode "automatically turns off after 2 hours if no interaction occurs", so a bank in low-current mode can stop before morning even when it is working correctly. Check your own bank's manual for its timer.
