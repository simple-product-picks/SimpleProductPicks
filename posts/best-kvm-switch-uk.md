# Best KVM Switch 2026

Most people who search for a KVM switch have first searched for a hub or a dock, because the three sound alike and do opposite jobs. A hub or a docking station adds ports, screens and power to one computer. A KVM switch does the reverse: it lets two computers share one keyboard, one mouse and, on most models, one monitor, so a single set of gear serves both machines and you swap between them with a button. If you only have one computer, no KVM will help you; if you have two, this is the box that clears the desk.

The first real decision is whether you need the screen switched at all. If your two computers already drive their own monitors and the only irritation is two keyboards and two mice, a plain USB switch is the honest, cheapest answer and there's nothing more to buy. If you want one shared monitor to follow whichever machine is active, you want a true KVM that switches video as well, and that is where most of the price and most of the spec sheet go.

The second decision is the connector. A KVM that switches video is built for one video standard, and HDMI and DisplayPort switches are not interchangeable, so match the switch to the port your monitor uses. Both video picks below are HDMI. After that it comes down to how many screens you switch and how high they go, and the three picks here spread neatly across exactly those choices. ## FAQ

**Q: What's the difference between a KVM switch, a USB hub and a docking station?**
A: They point in opposite directions. A hub or a dock expands one computer, adding ports, another screen or more power down a single cable. A KVM goes the other way, letting two or more computers take turns on one keyboard, mouse and often one monitor at the press of a button. With a single machine you want a hub or a dock, and our [USB-C hub versus docking station](usb-c-hub-vs-docking-station.html) guide covers those.

**Q: Do I need a KVM that switches video, or just a USB switch?**
A: If both computers already drive their own monitor and you only want to share the keyboard, mouse and USB gear, a USB switch like the budget pick is the cheaper, simpler answer and there's no display to re-detect on a swap. If you want one shared monitor to follow the active computer, you need a video KVM like the overall or premium pick.

**Q: Will an HDMI KVM work with a DisplayPort monitor?**
A: No. A video KVM is built for one standard, and HDMI and DisplayPort aren't interchangeable, so match the switch to the port your monitor uses. Both video picks here are HDMI; a DisplayPort-only monitor needs a DisplayPort switch instead.

**Q: Does a KVM switch add lag or lower my refresh rate?**
A: Short version: the switch only caps your refresh rate if its stated video ceiling or the cables sit below your monitor's mode, so match all three and nothing drops. The longer answer, including what actually happens the moment you swap between machines, is its own guide: [does a KVM switch add lag](kvm-switch-latency-explained.html).

**Q: How many computers can a KVM switch connect?**
A: All three picks here switch between two computers, which is by far the most common setup, a work machine and a personal one, say. Switches for four or more computers exist, but they're a different class of box; for two, these cover it.
