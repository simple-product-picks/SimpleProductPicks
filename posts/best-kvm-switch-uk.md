# Best KVM Switch 2026

A KVM switch lets two computers share one keyboard, one mouse and, on most models, one monitor, with a button to swap between them. It's the reverse of a hub or a docking station, which adds ports and screens to a single computer; with one machine, our [USB-C hub versus docking station](usb-c-hub-vs-docking-station.html) guide is the one you want.

Three things decide which switch: whether the screen needs switching at all, how many monitors you run, and the video input on your monitor. Each video KVM here is built for one standard, and HDMI and DisplayPort aren't interchangeable, so the connector has to match. The overall and premium picks are HDMI; the fourth pick is DisplayPort.

## FAQ

**Q: Will an HDMI KVM work with a DisplayPort monitor?**
A: Not directly. A DisplayPort-only screen needs the DisplayPort pick above. Before you buy any video KVM, also check which video port each of your computers has.

**Q: Does a KVM switch add lag or lower my refresh rate?**
A: It only caps the refresh rate when its stated video ceiling, or the cables, sit below your monitor's mode. What happens during the swap itself is covered in [does a KVM switch add lag](kvm-switch-latency-explained.html).

**Q: How many computers can a KVM switch connect?**
A: Every pick here takes two, the usual work-plus-personal setup. Switches for four or more computers exist, but they're a different class of box.
