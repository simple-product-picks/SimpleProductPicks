# Charging A Laptop From A Power Bank

## What has to line up for it to work

Two things decide whether a power bank can charge your laptop, and both have to be true. The first is the laptop's own charging port: it has to take its power over USB-C, the same reversible connector a phone uses, rather than through a round barrel plug. The second is what the bank's USB-C port can actually deliver.

That second part is USB-C Power Delivery, usually shortened to PD. It's the handshake that lets a charger and a device agree on a higher voltage, and it's what carries the wattage a laptop needs. Anker's UK guide is plain about it: a bank works "as long as the power bank supports USB-C Power Delivery (PD) and matches your laptop's power requirements". A bank that only does basic USB output can't have that conversation, so it never ramps up to laptop wattage.

## The wattage your laptop asks for

A laptop draws much more power than a phone, and the figure it wants sets the whole decision. Anker's guide puts the range from "30W (20V/1.5A)" for "something like a MacBook Air" at the low end up to "100W (20V/5A) to power up quickly and efficiently" for high-performance machines.

Apple's own MacBook Air specs back up the low end. Apple lists a 30W minimum for the 13-inch Air and 35W for the 15-inch, and says both are "fast-charge capable with 70W or higher USB PD power source". So even a thin, light laptop wants around 30W to charge at all, and more to charge quickly, while a larger, high-performance machine sits nearer the 100W end. The number you actually need is the wattage printed on your laptop's own charger, and the bank has to meet or beat it.

## Why a phone power bank stalls a laptop

Most people come unstuck the same way. They already own a power bank, so they assume it will do the job. Usually it won't. Anker's guide spells out why: "Most older power banks only support basic USB output, typically 5V/2A (10W) or maybe 9V/2A (18W). That's fine for phones, but not nearly enough to power up a laptop."

Line the two numbers up and the shortfall speaks for itself. A phone bank offering 10W to 18W is being asked to feed a laptop that wants at least 30W, or closer to 100W to fill up properly. At best the laptop charges painfully slowly, or only tops up while it's asleep; at worst the port does nothing at all, because the bank can't deliver the wattage the laptop insists on before it will accept a charge.

## How to read the label before you buy

You can tell a laptop-capable bank from a phone bank on the box, without measuring anything. Look for two things. First, "USB-C Power Delivery" or "USB-C PD" named on the port, not just "USB-C output", which on a cheap bank can still be a trickle. Second, a wattage figure on that PD port that is at least as high as your laptop's charger, so 30W or more for a slim laptop and closer to 100W for a large one.

Capacity is the second question, not the first, but it matters. A laptop battery is far bigger than a phone's, so a small bank will barely move the needle. Anker's guide suggests starting at "20,000mAh or more" for a laptop, which is the size most laptop-capable banks are built around.

## When a power bank can't help

Not every laptop can be charged this way, so check your own port first. If your laptop still charges through a round barrel plug rather than a USB-C port, an ordinary power bank has nowhere to plug in. Anker's guide is clear on the fix: "You'll need a power bank designed specifically with an AC outlet or a DC barrel output that matches your laptop's voltage." Those exist, but they're a heavier, bulkier, separate class of product, closer to a portable mains socket than to a pocket bank, so it helps to know that going in.

## FAQ

**Q: Can any power bank charge a laptop?**
A: No. It needs a USB-C Power Delivery output at or above your laptop's wattage, which is usually 30W or more. Most phone power banks only manage 10W to 18W, which isn't enough to charge a laptop properly, and some won't charge it at all.

**Q: How many watts do I need to charge a laptop?**
A: Match the wattage on your laptop's own charger. Anker's UK guide spans about 30W for a small laptop like a MacBook Air up to 100W for a high-performance machine, and Apple lists a 30W minimum for the 13-inch MacBook Air. Meeting that number lets it charge; beating it lets it charge faster.

**Q: My laptop charges with a round plug, not USB-C. Can I still use a power bank?**
A: Not an ordinary one. A normal power bank charges a laptop over USB-C, so a laptop with a barrel-style port needs a bank with a built-in mains socket or a matching DC output. Those are larger and heavier, so check your laptop's port before buying.
