## USB-C network adapter, Ethernet dongle or Ethernet adapter: is there a difference?

No. Retailers and manufacturers use all three names for the same thing: a small device with a USB-C plug on one end and an RJ45 Ethernet socket on the other. Whatever it's called, what separates one from another is the speed class, whether your operating system picks it up on its own, and how well it is built for a laptop bag. If a single-purpose dongle is not enough and you want HDMI and USB-A from the same port, our [USB-C hub guide](best-usb-c-hub-uk.html) sorts hubs by how many ports you really need.

## Gigabit, 2.5G or 5G: which speed do you actually need?

Match the adapter to the slowest link in your chain, not the fastest number on the box. Ofcom puts the average UK home download at 285 Mbit/s in 2025, up from 223 Mbit/s in 2024: well under Gigabit's 1000 Mbit/s. On a line like that a Gigabit adapter carries everything the line has, and a 2.5G or 5G model gains you nothing on downloads, although wired still beats Wi-Fi for stability on video calls and gaming. The faster classes earn their keep in two cases: a full-fibre multi-gigabit package, or moving large files between machines on your own network through a 2.5G router or switch. If you're not sure, check your router: if its LAN ports are labelled 1G or 10/100/1000, a Gigabit adapter is all it can use.

## Ethernet to USB-C: how to connect it

1. Plug the adapter into a USB-C port that supports data. Most laptop ports do, but some charge-only ports on docks and monitors will not recognise it.
2. Run an Ethernet cable from the RJ45 socket on the adapter to a spare LAN port on your router, mesh node or switch. Any Cat5e or better cable handles Gigabit; Cat6 is the comfortable choice for 2.5G and above.
3. Wait a few seconds. Current versions of Windows and macOS pick up most adapters on their own and switch to the wired connection; some chipsets prompt a quick driver download the first time.
4. Turn Wi-Fi off while you are wired. Both systems prefer Ethernet when it is available, but switching Wi-Fi off stops the laptop hopping between the two.
5. Run a speed test to confirm the wired link.
