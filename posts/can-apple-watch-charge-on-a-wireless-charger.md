# Can An Apple Watch Charge On A Wireless Charger?

## A phone's wireless charger won't charge the watch

The short version is the one a lot of product pages skip: an Apple Watch will not charge on an ordinary phone wireless charger. A flat Qi pad or a MagSafe phone puck is built to push power into the back of a phone, and the watch cannot take a charge that way. It charges instead from a small magnetic charger that snaps onto the back of the case, so the two are not interchangeable.

Apple says as much in its own support guidance. When a watch will not charge, the first instruction is to "Make sure you're using the Apple Magnetic Charging Cable or USB-C Magnetic Fast Charging Cable that came with your watch." A general phone pad is not on the list.

## What Apple actually lists as a charger for it

Apple names a short set of chargers for the Apple Watch: the Magnetic Charging Cable or USB-C Magnetic Fast Charging Cable in the box, the Apple Watch Magnetic Charging Dock, and the MagSafe Duo Charger. It also recognises "a WPT-compliant charger purchased in China or Indonesia", a region-specific exception rather than anything on a UK or US shelf.

Every one of those is a magnetic charger made to sit against the watch. So the honest way to put it is not that the watch refuses all wireless charging, but that Apple lists only its own magnetic chargers, plus that regional exception; a general-purpose phone pad is not among them.

## Fast charging tightens the requirements again

Charging the watch at all is one bar; fast-charging it is a higher one. Apple says "Fast charging is supported on Apple Watch Series 7 and later, Apple Watch SE 3, and Apple Watch Ultra and later", that "Fast charging requires an Apple USB-C Magnetic Fast Charging Cable", and that the cable needs "a comparable third-party USB-C power adapter that supports USB Power Delivery (USB-PD) of 5W or greater." It also notes that "WPT-compliant chargers don't support fast charging", so even the wireless charger Apple does recognise only tops the watch up at the ordinary rate. An older watch, or the wrong cable, charges at that ordinary rate whatever you feed it.

## FAQ

**Q: Will the Apple Watch charger charge my iPhone?**
A: No, and Apple does not list it for that. The watch's magnetic puck is shaped to the back of the watch, while an iPhone takes power from a flat Qi or MagSafe pad. The two chargers are not interchangeable in either direction.

**Q: Which Apple Watch models fast charge, and what do they need?**
A: Apple says fast charging is supported on "Apple Watch Series 7 and later, Apple Watch SE 3, and Apple Watch Ultra and later", and that it "requires an Apple USB-C Magnetic Fast Charging Cable" fed by a USB-C power adapter of 5W or greater. Earlier watches, or the older non-USB-C cable, charge at the standard rate.

**Q: Can one charger do both my iPhone and my Apple Watch?**
A: Yes, but through two separate charging areas rather than one shared pad. A 3-in-1 stand gives each device its own spot, and Apple's own MagSafe Duo charges "your iPhone 12, iPhone 13, or iPhone 14 and your Apple Watch simultaneously". On any such charger, check that the watch spot is a real fast-charge module and not a slow generic pad.

**Q: Does the Apple Watch come with a charger?**
A: It comes with the magnetic charging cable that Apple tells you to use. The power source behind it is yours to supply: Apple's guidance points to a USB-C power adapter that supports USB Power Delivery of 5W or greater.
