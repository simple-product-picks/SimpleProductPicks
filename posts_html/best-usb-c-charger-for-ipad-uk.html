Open a new iPad and you get the tablet and a cable, and nothing else. Apple's own spec sheets for the iPad and the iPad Air list a "USB-C Charge Cable (1 metre)" in the box and no power adapter at all, so the plug is now yours to buy. The awkward part is that almost any USB-C charger will do the job, which makes "which one" a harder question than it sounds. More watts charge an iPad faster, but only up to the point the tablet itself will draw, and Apple prints a hard wattage figure for exactly one model: the iPad Pro (M5), which its support page says needs "a USB-C compliant, 60 W or higher power adapter" to fast charge. Every other iPad is comfortable on far less. The three picks below cover the sensible range, from a plug that quietly does the one job to one that runs a laptop as well.

FAQ

Q: Do new iPads come with a charger? A: No. Apple's spec sheets list only a USB-C charge cable in the box for the iPad and the iPad Air, so the plug is something you now buy yourself. That is the whole reason this guide exists.

Q: What wattage charger does an iPad need? A: Any USB-C Power Delivery charger will charge an iPad, and more watts is faster only up to what the tablet itself will draw. Apple publishes a hard figure for just one model, the iPad Pro (M5) at 60W or higher; for a standard iPad, an Air or a mini, something in the 20W to 35W range is plenty.

Q: Is 20W enough for an iPad? A: For a standard iPad or an iPad mini, yes, at a steady pace. It is the entry point rather than the quickest option, and it cannot hit the fast-charge figure Apple quotes for the iPad Pro, which needs 60W.

Q: Can one charger do my iPad and my iPhone? A: Yes. The 35W pick adds a USB-A port and the 65W pick has three ports, so either charges an iPad and a phone at once. If the iPhone is the main thing you are charging, our iPhone charger guide picks for that instead.

Q: Will a 65W charger harm my iPad? A: No. The iPad and the charger agree the power between them over USB-C Power Delivery, and the tablet never draws more than it is built to take, so a 65W plug is as safe as a 20W one. A higher-wattage charger just reaches the iPad's own ceiling sooner.
