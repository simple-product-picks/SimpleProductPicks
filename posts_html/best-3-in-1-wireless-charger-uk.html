A 3-in-1 wireless charger puts an iPhone, an Apple Watch and AirPods on one base with one plug in the wall. Two things decide whether it's any good. The first is the watch spot: look for a stated Apple Watch charge time, and all three picks below quote one. The second is the phone pad's class. Apple says many Qi-certified chargers charge an iPhone "at rates up to 7.5 watts". The Wireless Power Consortium says Qi2 brought 15W, and its newer Qi2 25W tier, which Belkin labels Qi2.2, goes further. So the real choice here is how fast you want the phone pad, and whether you want a cooling fan to hold that speed.

FAQ

Q: Do I need a special charger to fast-charge an Apple Watch? A: Yes, in that the watch charges on its own spot, not on the phone pad; can an Apple Watch charge on a wireless charger explains why. For speed, go by the maker's quoted watch charge time, which each pick above gives.

Q: What's the difference between Qi2 15W and Qi2.2 25W? A: Both are wireless-charging standards from the Wireless Power Consortium, which says "Qi2 25W builds on the convenience and energy efficiency of Qi2 while delivering nearly 70% more charging power for faster charging."

Q: Will it charge my AirPods as well? A: Yes, the third spot is for the earbuds. Anker describes its earbuds area as working "not only for AirPods but also for other earbuds that support wireless charging".
