A 3-in-1 wireless charger is meant to clear the nightstand: iPhone, Apple Watch and AirPods on one base, one plug in the wall. The trouble is that the headline "15W" tells you almost nothing about the two things that actually decide whether it's any good. The first is the watch. A proper station carries a dedicated fast-charge module for it; a cheaper one hides a slow puck that trickles for hours. The second is the phone pad's class. Apple says an ordinary Qi charger tops out at up to 7.5 watts on an iPhone; the Wireless Power Consortium says Qi2 brought a real 15W; and the newer 25W tier, which Belkin brands Qi2.2 and the Consortium calls Qi2 25W, climbs higher still. All three picks below clear the watch test, so what is left is simpler: how fast you want the phone pad, and whether the extra speed is worth active cooling and the outlay. FAQ

Q: Do I need a special charger to fast-charge an Apple Watch? A: Effectively yes. The watch needs a dedicated fast-charge module, not a generic Qi puck, or it trickles slowly. Every pick here has one: Belkin quotes the budget station at 0% to 80% in 45 minutes and the premium at 0% to 80% in 30 minutes, and Anker quotes the overall pick at 0% to 47% in 30 minutes. That fast-charge module is the single biggest thing separating a good 3-in-1 from a cheap one.

Q: What's the difference between Qi2 15W and Qi2.2 25W? A: They're both wireless-charging standards. The Wireless Power Consortium says Qi2 brought a faster 15W, and that Qi2 25W (the label makers also print as Qi2.2) delivers nearly 70% more charging power. The important part: a 25W pad only helps if your iPhone supports the 25W class. An older phone charges at its own lower ceiling, so the faster pad just sits idle under it.

Q: Do these come with a plug, or do I supply my own? A: All three include a mains adapter, which is worth knowing because plenty of 3-in-1s ship cable-only and quietly need a separate fast plug. Anker lists a 40W charger with the overall pick, Belkin a 36W supply with the budget station and a 45W one with the premium. A small phone plug would starve any of them. Do still check the listing for what's in the box.

Q: Will it charge my AirPods as well? A: Yes, the third spot is a wireless pad for the earbuds. Anker, for example, describes a large non-slip earbuds charging area that works not only for AirPods but also for other earbuds that support wireless charging, so any case with wireless charging should sit happily on it.
