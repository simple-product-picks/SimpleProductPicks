If a card copy feels slow, the reader is rarely the thing holding it up. The limit is almost always the card's own bus. The SD Association puts UHS-I at up to 104MB/s and UHS-II at up to 312MB/s, and the jump comes from hardware you can see: UHS-II adds a second row of pins so it can move data down two lanes instead of one. The catch is that both ends have to be UHS-II. Put a UHS-I card in a UHS-II reader and you get UHS-I speed, because there is no second row of pins on the card for the reader to talk to. So the first question is not which reader is fastest, it's which card you actually shoot.

The second question is what you are plugging into. A laptop is happy with a plain reader. A phone or an iPad has one port, and the moment a reader is in it there is nowhere left for a charger, so the reader that also passes power through earns its place. And an older camera with a CompactFlash card in it needs a reader that still has a CF slot at all. The three picks below answer those three situations. If what you actually want is one dongle that reads cards and drives a monitor and a wired network from a laptop desk, that is a hub, not a reader, and our USB-C hub with SD card reader guide is the honest place to go instead.

FAQ

Q: Why is my SD card copy so slow? A: Almost always the card, not the reader. A card's speed is set by its bus: the SD Association puts UHS-I at up to 104MB/s and UHS-II at up to 312MB/s. A fast reader cannot lift a UHS-I card above the UHS-I ceiling, so if copies feel slow the usual fix is a faster card, not a faster reader.

Q: Will a UHS-II reader make my cards faster? A: Only if the cards are UHS-II as well. UHS-II works by adding a second row of pins for a second data lane, and a UHS-I card does not have them, so it reads at UHS-I speed in any reader. The UGREEN pick is worth it if you shoot UHS-II cards and wasted on ordinary ones.

Q: Can I read a card on my phone or iPad and charge it at the same time? A: Yes, with the right reader. A phone or iPad has one port, so a plain reader blocks the charger. The JSAUX overall pick adds a USB-C charging port for exactly this, so the device stays powered while a card copies.

Q: Do any of these read CompactFlash? A: The BENFEI budget pick does. Its listing reads SD, microSD, Memory Stick and CompactFlash, which makes it the one here for an older DSLR that still shoots CF. The other two are SD and microSD only.

Q: Should I buy a card reader or a hub with a card slot? A: A reader if cards are the whole job and you want something pocketable.
