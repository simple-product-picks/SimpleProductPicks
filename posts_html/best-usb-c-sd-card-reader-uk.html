If a card copy feels slow, check the card before the reader. The card's own bus sets the limit, and UHS-II gets its extra speed from a second row of pins that both ends need, so a UHS-II card only goes past the UHS-I ceiling in a UHS-II reader. Our UHS-I vs UHS-II explainer shows how to tell the two apart. So start with the cards you shoot, then the device you plug into: a phone or iPad has one port, and once a reader is in it there's nowhere left for the charger unless the reader passes power through.

FAQ

Q: Why is my SD card copy so slow? A: The SD Association puts UHS-I at up to 104MB/s and UHS-II at up to 312MB/s, so a UHS-I card stays under 104MB/s in any reader.

Q: Will these work with an iPhone or iPad? A: JSAUX's listing names iPhone 16 models and iPad Pro, and BENFEI's names the iPhone 15 series. UGREEN's title names no phone or tablet, so check its listing for your model first. For what an iPad does with the card once it is connected, the Files app versus the Photos Import screen and the formats it will read, see Can you use an SD card on an iPad?.

Q: Should I buy a card reader or a hub with a card slot? A: A reader if cards are the whole job. A hub if the same dongle also has to drive a monitor, a wired network or charging at a desk, and our USB-C hub guide covers those.
