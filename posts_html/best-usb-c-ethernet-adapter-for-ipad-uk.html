Will a USB-C Ethernet adapter work with my iPad?

Yes, on any iPad with a USB-C port. Apple's support page for the iPad's USB-C port lists USB to Ethernet adapters among the accessories the port connects to, and names the USB-C iPads: iPad Pro (M4 and M5), the 11-inch and 12.9-inch iPad Pro models back to 2018, iPad Air (M2, M3 and M4) and the 4th and 5th generation, iPad mini (A17 Pro) and 6th generation, iPad (A16) and iPad (10th generation). If your iPad is on that list, a plain USB-C Ethernet adapter works with no extra software.

The one thing worth checking is that the socket carries data rather than power alone. Every USB-C iPad in Apple's list has a data-capable port built in, so this only comes up if you route the adapter through a dock or monitor whose downstream socket is wired for charging only. A quick test: if that socket reads a USB flash drive, it will run an Ethernet adapter too.

Do you need gigabit, or is that overkill on an iPad?

Gigabit is far more than an iPad will ever pull from a home broadband line, which is why all three picks here are gigabit and none of them will bottleneck your connection. There is no iPad case for the faster 2.5G or 5G adapters: an iPad is a client device, not a file server, so the extra speed class would sit idle. The only thing that can hold a wired adapter below its rating is the USB port it plugs into, and our explainer on whether a USB-C Ethernet adapter slows your internet walks through that in full. For an iPad you can skip the worry: the USB-C port on every model in Apple's list is quick enough for gigabit.

What if you need to charge the iPad at the same time?

This is the one real limitation of a plain adapter. A USB-C iPad has a single port, so an Ethernet adapter takes the only socket you would otherwise charge from. For a quick sync or a call that is no problem, but for a long working session on a wired desk you have two options: an adapter with pass-through charging, or a USB-C hub that adds Ethernet alongside a charging input and other ports. A hub is the more flexible answer for a desk setup, and our USB-C hub with Ethernet guide compares the ones worth buying. If wired networking on the move is all you are after, a single-purpose adapter stays the lighter, cheaper choice.

How to connect an Ethernet cable to an iPad

  1. Plug the adapter's USB-C end into your iPad's USB-C port.
  2. Connect an Ethernet cable between the adapter's RJ45 socket and a free port on your router or switch. A basic Cat5e lead is enough for gigabit.
  3. Give it a second. iPadOS detects the wired connection automatically and starts using it.
  4. To be certain the iPad is on the cable and not Wi-Fi, open Settings and turn Wi-Fi off; iPadOS keeps the Ethernet link live.
  5. If the connection does not appear, try a different cable or LAN port, and make sure the adapter is in the iPad's own port rather than a charge-only port on a dock.

FAQ

Q: Can any iPad use a USB-C Ethernet adapter? A: Any iPad with a USB-C port can. Apple lists USB to Ethernet adapters among the accessories that port supports, covering the iPad Pro, iPad Air, iPad mini and standard iPad models that use USB-C. Older iPads with a Lightning port are not covered by this guide.

Q: Do I need an app or driver to use one? A: No. iPadOS handles a standard USB-C Ethernet adapter on its own; plug it in, connect the cable, and the wired link comes up without any software.

Q: Is gigabit enough for an iPad, or should I buy a 2.5G adapter? A: Gigabit is plenty. An iPad will not use more than a gigabit link gives it, so a 2.5G or 5G adapter adds cost without adding any real speed on a tablet.

Q: Can I charge my iPad while it is connected by Ethernet? A: Not with a plain adapter, because it uses the iPad's only USB-C port. For that you want an adapter with pass-through charging or a USB-C hub that combines Ethernet with a charging input.

Q: Why is my wired iPad connection slower than expected? A: The adapter itself is rarely the cause. Check the router LAN port, the cable, and whether the adapter is in the iPad's own port rather than a charge-only one; our speed explainer covers the ceilings in detail.

Q: Will one of these work through a docking station instead? A: It can, but if you are using a dock you may not need a separate adapter at all: many USB-C hubs and docks include Ethernet already, which is often the tidier answer for a fixed desk.
