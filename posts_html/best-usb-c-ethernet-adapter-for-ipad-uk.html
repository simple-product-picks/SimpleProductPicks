Will a USB-C Ethernet adapter work with my iPad?

Yes, on any iPad with a USB-C port. Apple's support page for that port lists "USB to Ethernet adapters" among the accessories it connects to, so this is a supported setup, not a hack.

Do you need gigabit, or is that overkill on an iPad?

Both picks are gigabit. The faster 2.5G and 5G classes suit machines that move large files across a home network, like a laptop or a NAS, while an iPad is a client device, so we haven't picked one for it here. The port an adapter plugs into can also hold it below its rating, and our explainer on whether a USB-C Ethernet adapter slows your internet walks through that.

What if you need to charge the iPad at the same time?

A USB-C iPad has a single port, so an Ethernet adapter takes the socket you'd otherwise charge from. That's fine for a quick sync or a call, but for a long session at a desk you want a USB-C hub that adds Ethernet alongside a charging input. Our USB-C hub guide says which picks carry an Ethernet socket.

How to connect an Ethernet cable to an iPad

  1. Plug the adapter's USB-C end into your iPad's USB-C port.
  2. Connect an Ethernet cable between the adapter's RJ45 socket and a free port on your router or switch. A basic Cat5e lead is enough for gigabit.
  3. To be certain the iPad is on the cable and not Wi-Fi, open Settings and turn Wi-Fi off; iPadOS keeps the Ethernet link live.
  4. If the connection doesn't appear, try a different cable or router port.
