A Surface Pro looks like a hard charging question and turns out to be a simple one. Microsoft publishes a per-model table of what each Surface wants over USB-C, and the highest recommended figure on it, for the Surface Pro 9, X, 8 and the 11th-Edition and 13-inch models, is 60 watts. The 12-inch Pro asks for 45. So a single 65W USB-C Power Delivery charger covers every USB-C Surface Pro Microsoft lists, with a few watts to spare.

Two things are worth knowing before you buy. Microsoft says a charger of 60W or higher turns a drained Surface on the instant you plug it in, while a lower-wattage or USB-A charger "may charge slowly or not at all". And a Surface Pro 6 or older cannot take a USB-C charger at all, because it charges only through the Surface Connect port.

So once you are past 65W, speed stops being the thing to shop for. What is left to decide is whether you want a cable built in, how many other devices share the plug, and whether you will ever need 100 watts. All three picks below supply at least the 65W a Surface Pro asks for.

FAQ

Q: What wattage charger does a Surface Pro need? A: Microsoft's table sets the bar. The most demanding USB-C models, the Surface Pro 9, X, 8 and the 11th-Edition and 13-inch Pros, recommend 60 watts; the 12-inch Pro asks for 45. A single 65W USB-C PD charger clears all of them, and Microsoft adds that a charger of 60W or higher turns a drained Surface on the moment you plug it in.

Q: Will a 100W charger charge my Surface Pro faster than a 65W one? A: No. The Surface draws up to its own recommended wattage, 60 watts at most, and no more, so a 65W and a 100W charger fill it at the same speed. The only reason to buy 100W is to charge other devices from the same plug at the same time.

Q: Can I charge an older Surface Pro over USB-C? A: The Surface Pro 7 and 7+ charge over USB-C at 60W, though without fast charging. The Surface Pro 6 and anything earlier cannot: Microsoft lists them as Surface Connect only, so a USB-C charger will not charge them. Check your exact model against Microsoft's list before you buy.

Q: Do I need a special cable? A: You need a USB-C to USB-C cable rated for the wattage you want, and the charger and Surface negotiate the rest over USB-C Power Delivery. The overall pick has a retractable cable built in; the budget and premium picks do not, so you supply your own. Our USB-C fast-charging cable guide covers what to look for.
