Microsoft publishes a per-model table of what each Surface Pro wants over USB-C, and the highest recommended figure on it is 60 watts, for the Surface Pro 9, X and 8, the Pro 9 with 5G and the 11th Edition. The 12-inch Pro asks for 45. So a single 65W USB-C Power Delivery charger covers every USB-C Surface Pro Microsoft lists, with a few watts to spare.

Microsoft also says that if the battery is drained and the charger is 60W or higher, the Surface "will instantly turn on when you plug it in", while a lower-wattage or USB-A charger "may charge slowly or not at all". A Surface Pro 6 or older can't take a USB-C charger at all: Microsoft lists them as Surface Connect only.

With 65W settled, the choice comes down to whether you want a cable built in and how many other devices share the plug.

FAQ

Q: Can I charge a Surface Pro 7 over USB-C? A: Yes. Microsoft's table lists the Surface Pro 7 and 7+ at a 60W minimum with no fast charging, so a 65W charger works but won't fast-charge them. Check your exact model against Microsoft's list before you buy.

Q: Do I need a special cable? A: For a plug without a built-in cable, use a USB-C to USB-C cable rated for the wattage you want; the charger and Surface agree the rest over USB-C Power Delivery. Our USB-C fast-charging cable guide covers what to look for.
