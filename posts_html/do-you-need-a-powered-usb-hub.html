Do You Need A Powered USB Hub?

Plain English • No sponsored placements
Written and checked by Simple Product Picks · last updated October 2026
✓ Plain-English explainer ✓ No sponsored placements

The short answerIt comes down to what you plug in, not to a powered hub being inherently better. A bus-powered hub shares one upstream port's power budget across everything connected to it; Wikipedia's USB hub article describes a USB port's current as allocated in units of 100 mA up to 500 mA per port, which covers low-draw peripherals like a keyboard, mouse, flash drive or controller. The moment a hungry device asks for more than that budget can spare (Anker gives the example of running several external hard drives at once), a self-powered hub that supplies full power to every port earns its place. The tell that you have crossed the line, as CMD's advice centre puts it, is devices failing to operate correctly, frequent disconnections, or reduced performance.

Where a hub's power comes from

An unpowered hub is bus-powered. Anker puts it plainly: "bus-powered means that the USB hub takes power from your PC or laptop to operate", so "the power output from a single USB port gets distributed all over the hub". A powered hub is different: UGREEN says it "uses its own external power adapter to supply electricity to connected devices", and Wikipedia's USB hub article says a self-powered hub "can therefore provide full power (up to 500 mA) to every port". Those 500 mA figures are the USB 2.0 baseline; USB 3 and USB-C ports can supply more, but a bus-powered hub still has only that one port's supply to share.

When a bus-powered hub is all you need

Anker is clear that "a powered USB hub is not needed when you only need the extension to connect low-power peripherals such as USB flash drives, keyboards, mice, or game controllers". CMD's advice centre draws the same line for "keyboards, mice, and USB flash drives". If that's your desk, a powered model only adds a second cable and a mains plug.

When you actually need the mains adapter

Anker's reason is simple: "external hard drives require more power than keyboards and mice". UGREEN names "external hard drives, printers, and scanners" as the high-power devices that want a powered hub, and CMD adds "monitors" to that list. If your plans include a desk of storage or any of those, buy the powered version from the start and skip the troubleshooting.

How to tell your hub has run out of power

CMD's trigger is "too many power-demanding devices" on an unpowered hub, so a drive that works alone but drops out once a second one goes in fits the pattern. Wikipedia adds that the computer may tell you outright: "if a device requires more units of current than the port it is plugged into is able to supply, the operating system usually reports this to the user".

Two fixes work. Move the demanding device onto a port on the computer itself, which gives it that port's full supply, or switch to a powered hub so every port has its own.

FAQ

Q: Can I just add another unpowered hub to get more power? A: No. A second bus-powered hub in the chain still draws on the same single upstream port, so it splits that one port's supply further rather than adding any.
