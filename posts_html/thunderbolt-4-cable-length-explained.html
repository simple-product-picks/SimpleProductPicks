Thunderbolt 4 Cable Length Limits

Plain English • No sponsored placements
Written and checked by Simple Product Picks · last updated October 2026
✓ Plain-English explainer ✓ No sponsored placements

The short answer'Thunderbolt 4' takes the length trap off the table for any run of 2 metres or less. Intel states that 'Thunderbolt 4 cables provide top transfer speeds at up to 2 meters', and Eaton adds that under Thunderbolt 4 'passive cables will be able to transmit up to 2 m at 40 Gb/s' - the fix for Thunderbolt 3, where Eaton says a passive lead fell from the full 40 Gb/s at half a metre to 20 Gb/s at 2 metres. Past 2 metres you need an active cable, which Plugable describes as carrying Intel circuits called 'retimers' that boost the signal, so for a desk a 1m or 2m certified lead is the whole answer and only a run across the room needs active.

Why length used to cost you speed

On Thunderbolt 3, a longer lead meant slower data unless you bought a different kind of cable. Eaton's explainer sets out the old rule: "A passive Thunderbolt 3 cable can only transmit at the full 40 Gb/s data rate over a distance of 0.5 m (18 inches)." To hold that speed further out, "for the maximum 40 Gb/s throughput up to 2 meters, you'll need an active Thunderbolt 3 cable." So a passive 2m Thunderbolt 3 lead didn't fail outright. It ran at half speed.

What Thunderbolt 4 changed

Thunderbolt 4 made length a non-issue up to 2 metres. Intel, which owns the standard, says "Thunderbolt 4 ports have a 40Gbps (gigabits per second) bidirectional bandwidth", and a Thunderbolt 4 cable up to 2m carries all of it. You no longer need a bandwidth-versus-length chart, or to work out whether a short cable is passive or active. Check that it's a Thunderbolt 4 cable and no longer than 2m.

Passive, active, and when the difference bites

A passive cable is a plain set of wires. An active one has electronics built in to keep the signal clean over distance: in Plugable's words, "specialized Intel circuits, called retimers, along the data path that act as relay stations to boost the signal". That's what buys the extra length, and you only need it past 2 metres.

How long a cable do you actually need

If the run from your laptop to its dock, monitor, stand or shelf is 2 metres or less, a 1m or 2m Thunderbolt 4 cable covers it. You only need an active cable when the gear is genuinely far apart: a dock across the room, a display wall, or a lead routed through a desk grommet and along a cable tray. Measure the run before you buy.

FAQ

Q: Will an old active Thunderbolt 3 cable work for everything? A: Not quite. Plugable points out that "you can get an active Thunderbolt 3 cable, but it only transmits Thunderbolt data, not USB," so it can leave an ordinary USB device dead. Its advice for a long lead: "If you need an active cable, stick with Thunderbolt 4, it's more versatile."
