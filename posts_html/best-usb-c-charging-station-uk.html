A desktop charging station earns its place when one mains lead can power everything on a desk at once - the laptop, the phone, a tablet, the earbuds - instead of a wall of separate plugs. What decides which one to buy is total power before port count: a station's wattage is shared across every port in use, so the more you plug in, the less each device gets. Size up the total first, then the ports.

All three picks here use GaN, which Anker describes as delivering "higher power in a smaller, more efficient package". It only moves power, though: for a screen and files as well, a USB-C docking station is the box to compare. The picks are a 200W eight-port all-rounder, a 108W four-port station with USB-A for older kit, and a 250W six-port step-up for a desk that runs two laptops.

FAQ

Q: How much power does a charging station need? A: Add up the wattage each device's own charger is rated for, across the devices you'll charge at the same time, and pick a station whose total covers them; then check the listing's per-port figure for your laptop's port. A second laptop on top is where the 200W or 250W stations earn their place.

Q: Can I take one travelling? A: They're built for a desk: UGREEN and Belkin both call theirs a desktop charger. For a bag you want a fold-flat brick instead; our 65W GaN charger guide and our 100W GaN charger guide cover the portable options.

Q: Do I need a special cable? A: For the higher-power ports, yes: a charger only delivers its full wattage down a cable rated to carry it. Our fast-charging cable guide explains what to look for.
