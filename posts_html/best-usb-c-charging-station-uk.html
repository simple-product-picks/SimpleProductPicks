A desktop charging station earns its place when one mains lead can power everything on a desk at once - the laptop, the phone, a tablet, the earbuds - instead of a wall of separate plugs. The question that actually decides which one to buy is not how many ports it has but how much total power it can hand out: plug a laptop, a phone and a tablet into a weak station and the laptop quietly drops to a trickle. So the first thing to size up is total wattage, then the number of ports.

A charging station is a stationary thing. It has a fixed mains lead and lives on the desk, which is the opposite of a fold-flat travel charger you throw in a bag - if that is what you want, our travel GaN charger guide is the better page. A station trades portability for ports and total power, and the good ones use GaN internals to do it without taking over the desk.

One more distinction worth getting straight: a charging station only moves power. It does not carry your screen or your files the way a USB-C docking station does, so if you need video and data as well, that is a different box. The three picks below are all about power: a 200W eight-port all-rounder for a busy desk, a cheaper 108W four-port station with USB-A for older kit, and a 250W six-port step-up for a desk that runs two laptops.

FAQ

Q: How much power does a charging station need? A: It depends on the desk. One laptop on its own needs only modest power; add a phone, a tablet and earbuds charging at the same time and you want more total headroom, or the laptop slows to a trickle. The 108W Belkin suits a laptop and a phone or two, the 200W UGREEN covers a busy desk, and the 250W Anker is built for two laptops at once.

Q: What's the difference between a charging station and a docking station? A: A charging station only moves power - it charges several devices from one mains lead. A docking station also carries video and data, so it can add monitors, Ethernet and USB drives. If you need a screen and files as well as power, our USB-C docking station guide is the page to read.

Q: Can I take one travelling? A: Not really - these have a fixed mains lead and are built to live on a desk. For a bag you want a fold-flat brick instead; our travel GaN charger guide and our 100W GaN charger guide cover the portable options.

Q: Do I need a special cable? A: For the higher-power ports, yes - a charger only delivers its full wattage down a cable rated to carry it, so a cheap thin cable can cap a fast charge. Our fast-charging cable guide explains what to look for.

Q: Is USB-A still worth having? A: If you own older keyboards, mouse dongles or cables, yes. The budget Belkin keeps two USB-A ports for exactly that; check any station's port mix against what you actually plug in before you buy.
