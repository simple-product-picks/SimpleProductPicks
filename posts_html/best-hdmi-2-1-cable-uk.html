If a new console or graphics card will not switch on its sharpest picture mode, the cable is the easiest part of the setup to rule out and the first thing to check. The good news is that the decision is simpler than the shelf makes it look. The only thing that really matters is whether the cable is a certified Ultra High Speed HDMI cable, because that certification has to be tested for rather than just printed on a box. A certified cable is rated for the full 48Gbps of HDMI 2.1, and a dearer certified cable does not carry any more than that. So this is a short guide with two picks: one that names a little more headroom and reach, and one that does the same core job for less. What you are really paying for above the cheaper pick is length and fit, not speed.

How we chose

The certification is what everything else here turns on. The HDMI Forum rates a certified Ultra High Speed HDMI cable "for system configurations supporting up to 48Gbps maximum bandwidth", and that headroom is what carries the demanding modes a recent console or graphics card can send, such as 4K at 120Hz. A cable without that certification might manage it and might not, and a version number in a listing is only something a seller typed, so both picks here are certified and we did not consider anything that was not. After that the honest axes are length and fit, not speed: a longer certified cable is the one place more money genuinely buys you something useful, because a passive cable's grip on the full 48Gbps weakens with distance, so the sensible move is the shortest certified cable that reaches. A wall-mounted TV with little room behind it is the one case worth planning for, where a right-angle plug or a slightly longer run saves a strained cable. FAQ

Q: Do I really need an HDMI 2.1 cable, or will the one I have work? A: Only once a source actually sends 4K120 or 8K and your screen can show it. Below that an existing High Speed cable holds nothing back, and the certified cable earns its place only when the kit at both ends can use the extra bandwidth.

Q: Is a more expensive HDMI 2.1 cable better than a cheap certified one? A: Not for bandwidth. Both picks here carry the same 48Gbps certification, so neither is faster on the modes you will actually use. What more money buys is length or fit extras like a right-angle plug, which are comfort and fit rather than performance, so match the cable to your run rather than to its price.

Q: What length of HDMI 2.1 cable should I buy? A: The shortest one that comfortably reaches. Holding the full 48Gbps gets harder the longer a passive cable runs, so a short certified lead is the safe choice; buy the extra length only if you need it, and for a wall-mounted TV plan the route before you pick the size.
