Charging a laptop over USB-C

A laptop charges from one port, so read a bank's single-port figure, not its total; the top output row in the table gives both for the UGREEN, while the INIU 65W's listing does not say how its 65W splits across ports. For scale, Apple lists a "140W USB-C Power Adapter" for the 16-inch MacBook Pro's fastest charge, more than any of the three gives from one port. The lead matters too: a USB-C cable rated 60W stops at 3A, so above 60W you need one rated 100W or 240W, like the fast-charging USB-C cables we pick. FAQ

Q: Can I take a 20000mAh power bank on a flight? A: Yes, in your hand luggage: at about 74Wh (20000mAh x 3.7V / 1000), it sits under the 100Wh line. The UK CAA says power banks "must only be carried as carry-on baggage", and on US flights the FAA says spare lithium batteries, power banks included, "must be carried in carry-on baggage only", with each limited to "a rating of 100 watt hours (Wh) per battery" unless your airline approves up to two larger spares of up to 160 Wh. Airlines can set stricter limits, so check yours; our power bank flight rules guide covers using a bank on board, and our power banks for long flights guide picks for cabin use.

Q: How long does a 20000mAh power bank take to recharge? A: Anker's charging guide lists "8-10 hours" on a standard charger and "~4-6 hours" on a fast one for a 20,000mAh bank, not for the three banks here. Your wait is set by the bank's input rating and the charger you refill it from, so check the input watts on the listing and use a charger that can supply them; our guide to how long a power bank takes to charge shows how to speed it up.
