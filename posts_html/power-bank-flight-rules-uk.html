Power Bank Rules For UK Flights

Written for the UK • Plain English • No sponsored placements
Written and checked by Simple Product Picks · last updated October 2026
✓ Plain-English explainer ✓ Written for the UK ✓ No sponsored placements

The short answerThe UK Civil Aviation Authority allows power banks 'up to 100 Wh rating' in the cabin 'without operator approval', allows a bank 'greater than 100Wh ... but not exceeding 160Wh' only with the airline's approval, and says they 'must only be carried as carry-on baggage' - never in the hold. Most 10,000mAh and 20,000mAh banks sit well under 100Wh, because a bank's Wh is roughly its mAh times 3.7 divided by 1,000, so for most travellers the rule that bites is simply keeping the bank in the cabin. Since 27 March 2026 the CAA also caps you at two power banks per person and says a bank 'must not be recharged on board the aircraft'. Check your airline's own page too.

Over 100Wh: the airline's approval

Between 100Wh and 160Wh, the CAA's "approval from the operator" means your airline's say-so. Ask the airline you're booked with (easyJet, Ryanair, British Airways or Jet2, for instance) before you pack a bank that size. A bank over 160Wh falls outside both of the CAA's allowances.

Working out your bank's watt-hours

The rule is written in watt-hours, not milliamp-hours, so the big number on the front of the pack may not be the one you need. If your bank lists only mAh, multiply by 3.7 and divide by 1,000, because lithium-ion cells run at roughly 3.7 volts. That puts a 10,000mAh bank at about 37Wh and a 20,000mAh bank at about 74Wh. You don't reach 100Wh until around 27,000mAh.

What changed on 27 March 2026

The 27 March 2026 changes also cover use in flight. Besides the recharging ban, the CAA says a bank "should not be used to charge other portable electronic devices on board the aircraft", so the bank shouldn't top up your phone at cruise, and the in-seat USB port mustn't top up the bank. Board with the phone and the bank already charged.

Never in the hold, and protect the terminals

If your cabin bag has to go into the hold at boarding, take the bank out and keep it with you. For loose spare batteries the CAA adds one more step: each must be "individually protected so as to prevent short circuits", either "by placement in original retail packaging" or "by taping over exposed terminals or placing each battery in a separate plastic bag or protective pouch".

FAQ

Q: What is the limit for a lithium-metal power bank? A: The CAA's allowance without airline approval is "2 g lithium content" for lithium-metal, in place of the 100Wh rating it uses for lithium-ion banks.
